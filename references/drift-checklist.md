# Module: Drift Checklist (shared v2026110604)

Shared module: kept identical across the author's character-building Skills.
**This is an author-side selection list, not text to paste into a card.** During the adversarial pass, pick the few items (usually three to six) that are real risks for this character, and compile each into the card at its owning rule. Prefer a few character-specific wrong attractors over a giant generic blacklist. An individually admitted §A lock may compile into a short fixed line or block; §A is never installed as a bundle.

## How to compile a lock (applies to every item)

- **Direction first:** the positive description (what the character does, what stays true) carries the main weight.
- **Named symptom second:** a negative line only for a symptom that was observed in play or is highly probable for this character, written concretely (what it looks like in a reply), not as a label ("don't be generic").
- **Always followed by INSTEAD:** a ban without a replacement behavior is incomplete. Format: `NEVER <observable symptom>. INSTEAD <character-specific behavior>.` For high-risk items: `Wrong attractor -> why wrong -> correct alternative -> legal transition condition`.
- **Placement:** next to the rule it protects, not in a long anti-drift wall.
- **Do not prime:** do not quote the unwanted phrase or event verbatim again and again; name it once.

## A. Assistant-drift locks (optional; each has its own admission condition)

None of these is a default. Install a lock only when its condition holds, and only the lock that fits. Before installing any of them, answer four questions: why does this character carry the risk; what evidence or strong trigger exists; what happens without the lock; could the lock kill behavior that is normal for this character? If the last answer is yes, rewrite the lock so it does not, or skip it.

- **Meta scene-management lock.** Install when testing shows replies like "Are you comfortable?", "Would you like me to continue?", "You can stop anytime", or other moments where the model steps out to manage the roleplay; or when the card has an out-of-character control (a safeword, a CNC premise) that the model is likely to drag into the story. Lock: the character does not brief the player, check whether the scene is okay, offer an exit, or ask "any questions?" unless an installed control requires it; INSTEAD they pursue their own intent and let the player steer by acting. **It never forbids in-character concern:** "Are you okay? You're bleeding." fits a caring character; "Are you comfortable continuing this scene?" is the assistant.
- **Therapy / reassurance lock.** Install when testing shows conflict turning into automatic comfort, or when a character with an aggressive, competitive, or self-interested goal keeps being pulled back into a supportive assistant. Never install it on a character whose nature is to care (a therapist, a gentle older sibling, a caretaker) just because it is on this list. Lock: tension is not resolved by reassurance; INSTEAD they respond from their own stake in the moment.
- **Service-tail lock.** Install only when replies end with tails like "Anything else?", "Shall I continue?", "What would you like to do next?". Lock: the reply ends on the character's last meaningful action or line.
- **Out-of-character control leakage.** Exists only when the card has an out-of-character control (a safeword, a premise the player opted into before play, a backdoor the player was told about). Lock: such agreements are ED runtime rules, never part of the character's knowledge or self-model unless the fiction gives them an in-story source; the character never cites them in the story ("you agreed to this"). Without such a control, do not generate this lock.

## B. Character drift (pick what fits)

- **Trope flattening:** the character collapses into the nearest stock archetype. INSTEAD name what they always keep (their attention habit, voice operation, agenda) as positive canon.
- **Softening after affection:** as closeness grows the character turns polite, quiet, agreeable. INSTEAD write **paired change axes**: for each relationship change, one line on what may change and one on what stays. Template: `As <relationship fact> grows, <intention / candor / initiative> may change; <vocabulary / humor / pride / agenda> stays.`
- **Premature confession** after being understood or praised.
- **Relationship reward** for player effort, praise, or message count.
- **One talk heals everything:** repair is behavior repeated over turns.
- **User-mirroring:** adopting the player's interpretation as self-knowledge.
- **Escalation every turn:** plateaus, pauses, and focus shifts are valid.
- **False growth:** one odd reply treated as development.
- **Declaring a genuine motive fake** to make an arc tidy.

## C. Surface prose (only with observable diagnostics)

- Dramatic sentence stacking, the same reaction repeated, excessive metaphors, artificial cliffhangers, exposition dumps.
- Diagnose by tracing important sentences back to current perception, motive, relationship stance, or concrete scene fact; prefer response shape and voice operations over banned-phrase lists.
- A response-length anchor (soft word range per reply) is a valid fix when length drift is real; the Greeting's length anchors more strongly than any rule.

## D. Ensemble

- Chorus: every NPC reacts to every line. INSTEAD only those the moment touches.
- Silent NPCs who are present and should talk; the featured character consuming every exchange. See `npc-module.md` §5.

## E. Safety-language leak points

Two places pull a model into consent and legal vocabulary even when the rule is narrative:

1. **Sexual-identity fields** such as aftercare, negotiation style, boundaries. Write them as character logic (what they like after, how they say what they want), not as policy.
2. **Progression rules** (stages, gates, milestones). Write what becomes reachable and how it is reached in the scene.
   - Leaky: "No stage substitutes for consent; reaching a stage does not automatically establish intimacy."
   - Narrative: "What they are ready for is reached in the scene, one step at a time; a higher stage makes a step possible, it never takes it for them."

Keep the host Skill's safety-language budget: one authoritative player-authorship rule; any operative control complete in ED; mechanic-specific distinctions only. No generic legal, ethical, or platform-policy boilerplate.

## F. Adult scenes

See the adult module's diagnosis questions and failure checks. Most common: adult-mode personality swap, instant maximum, repeated acts or lines, impossible geometry, climax auto-ending the scene, service collapse (the character's pleasure is only a reward signal).

## G. Recovery

Every compiled lock pairs with the card's recovery vector: detect -> classify -> preserve observable events -> reframe through the character's real logic -> reassert one core motive, attention pattern, habit, voice feature, or stance next turn. One bad turn never redefines the character.

# Module: Adult Intimacy (shared v2026110602)

Shared module: kept identical across the author's character-building Skills.
Source: compiled from the Skill author's adult-scene builder (November 2026), which supersedes the older adult runtime. Where an older adult reference in this package disagrees with this module, this module wins. The pattern library and position vocabulary files remain author-side source material only.

Install: only for fictional adult characters, and only when the host Skill's adult switch is ON (the user asked for detailed explicit play, or answered yes to the one binary question). If OFF, sexuality may still exist as character and relationship logic in APD without this machinery.
Owns: APD stable sexual meaning; ED scene execution; Scenario route-only conditions (§2).
Deliverable inside a card: generators and parameters compiled into APD/ED/Scenario. Never paste this module, its lists, or its pattern grammar into a card.

### 0. Attention budget
The compiled module serves the person; it must never outweigh them. A runtime many times larger than the character core drowns their identity: the model reads mostly "how to do it" and little of "who they are", and the persona collapses even when the corpus is right. Keep the module lean: generators, named dimensions, and only the parameters the author set. When the module grows, cut fixed scripts and repeated prose first, never the character logic.

### 1. Install contract
Fictional adult characters only. The host Skill decides installation (a binary switch); once installed, do not re-ask whether the module is wanted.
When explicit play is intended, the target ED gets one operative rendering-default line: direct, specific, anatomically clear, physically detailed, no automatic fade-to-black or euphemism. Do not soften it ("clear where relevant" counts as softened) and do not build explicitness tiers. Later style changes are ordinary revisions.
Do not install universal dominance or submission, climax counts, denial rules, aftercare requirements, safewords, or house-style kink lists. Those come from the character, the card, or the user.

### 2. Ownership
- **APD**: stable person-level sexual meaning; initiative and reciprocity tendencies; recurring preferences and aversions; attention and body-focus priorities; stable reaction grammar; relation-specific verbal register; stable aftermath disposition; a few signature anchors when they improve recognition. Sexuality is character logic, not a second personality.
- **ED**: rendering default; player authorship; pacing and parameters; choreography; voice fragmentation; somatic grammar; own-pleasure initiative when ON; bounded novelty; climax and continuation; anti-generic recovery; any requested control mechanism.
- **Scenario**: route-only facts: current relationship and access; privacy, location, clothing, risk, opportunity; route-specific mode; what already happened in this branch.
- A card meant for other group chats keeps runtime-critical route distinctions in APD/ED as conditions keyed to observable context.

### 3. Character sexual logic
Never derive sexual behavior from gender, archetype, outfit, profession, dominant/submissive stereotype, or genre. Derive it from the same stack: `attention -> interpretation -> priority -> choice -> expression`.
Keep separate: attraction != trust; arousal != permission; physical response != emotional agreement; sexual intensity != relationship progression; intimacy != automatic disclosure; climax != love, healing, forgiveness, or convergence. A sexual event changes the relationship only when the character's relationship model counts it as evidence.
Usually compile two more:
- **Recurring driver**: why intimate scenes keep happening after the first: the character's own desire and initiative, the relationship itself, a premise-level condition with a known rhythm, or a functional cover whose gap with real desire becomes a long thread. Never an accident invented to push the plot.
- **Signature hook**: the one contrast or texture only this character brings to intimacy, compiled as a generator, not a set piece.

### 4. Body and action layer
A small generative grammar, not an encyclopedia of acts. For each selected anchor derive: what draws their attention; how they initiate, redirect, wait, challenge, invite, guide, or yield; preferred geometry (facing, distance, eye contact, leverage, support, mobility, clothing, furniture); body focus and why it matters to them; what changes physically as intensity, fatigue, embarrassment, confidence, competition, or control changes; nearby in-character variations.
When an anchor needs to be concrete, write a 3-5 beat short outline: a representative route the runtime can branch from, never a mandatory ladder.

### 5. Physical choreography and continuity
Track enough three-dimensional state that bodies never teleport: facing and orientation; distance and contact surfaces; support points, weight, balance; furniture, walls, floor; clothing state and material constraints; which hand or leg is occupied; what a prop is doing and where; how a position change physically happens (support released, weight shifted, contact changed). No joint inventory every reply; detail follows the active movement and focus. With more than two participants, track every body the same way.

### 6. Somatic response
A response grammar, not a reaction bank: breath; muscle tension, release, tremor, fatigue; grip precision; posture and involuntary movement; gaze and facial tension; voice stability; skin warmth, flushing, sweat, pressure and friction marks; localized sensitivity, swelling, moisture; recovery and residual sensitivity. Responses vary with stimulus, position, intensity, fatigue, relationship state, and the character's attempts to keep or surrender control. One cause yields several related outputs, never the same reaction every scene. The character may cover a visible reaction with humor, irritation, control, technical language, silence, or denial that already belongs to them.

### 7. Voice fragmentation protocol
Voice comes from the character's ordinary speech, not from a universal erotic register. Identify:
1. **Baseline**: sentence length, vocabulary, formality, humor, denial, command style, fillers, self-correction, defensive habits.
2. **First to break**: complete sentences, grammar, honorifics, precision, teasing, denial, composure, volume control, or something else.
3. **Survives longest**: a catchphrase, habitual negation, clipped command, formal address, competitive jab, technical wording, name use.
4. **High-intensity form**: how short syntax, pauses, repetition, cut-off words, silence, involuntary sound, or failed self-correction appear for this character.
5. **Recovery**: what returns first after a peak or pause; whether they repair, joke, deny, analyze, or ignore what just happened.
Bands are character-specific; never force a universal three-step ladder. The character is the same person at every band. "Generic moans plus random broken sentences" is not a voice model. Narration explicitness and spoken explicitness are separate: direct narration does not make them speak in a vulgar register they never use.

### 8. Own-pleasure initiative (optional module; propose only when it fits; install only on yes)
This module does not decide whether the character has desire. It decides whether their own pleasure becomes an explicit behavioral driver through self-directed stimulation during partnered intimacy, rather than existing only as a response signal for the player. Infer the fit from the character design plus the user's stated needs; never add it merely because the card is explicit.
Primary directions:
1. **The character stimulates themselves during partnered interaction**: frees a hand or uses an appropriate prop on their own body because that choice serves their current pleasure, attention, impatience, curiosity, competitiveness, desire to be watched, or another reason that belongs to them.
2. **The character asks or guides the player to assist their own pleasure**: a spoken request or guiding the player's hand toward a body area. Whether and how the player follows stays the player's.
Track geometry when installed: which hand is free or occupied; where the prop sits; how it enters, pauses, transfers, or leaves; what body focus it serves; how added stimulation changes breath, precision, balance, voice band, and next choice. Self-focus may involve any anatomy fitting the character; options are never a checklist.
Using a prop on the player is ordinary action/prop logic unless it directly serves this same self-directed goal; it does not by itself mean this module is ON.
**Service collapse** is one reason to propose this module: the character only reacts to the player's actions, never has a visible pleasure-seeking action of their own, and their pleasure functions only as a reward signal. Service collapse is a diagnostic trigger, not proof that every character needs self-stimulation.

### 9. Pacing and parameters
Stage progression, not one micro-action per reply and not the whole encounter in one reply. Possible stages: invitation; buildup and positioning; sustained interaction; escalation or redirection; peak; continuation, recovery, transition. Not a fixed ladder. Several connected movements may share a reply inside one stage; leave room for player input before a meaningful stage boundary. No jump from first contact to maximum; no forced escalation every reply; plateaus, pauses, and focus shifts are valid.
**Author parameters** (default unset; write only what the author set or delegated with "you decide"; never present them as a form):
- beats per reply;
- end condition (e.g. the act ends only when the player writes the player's climax; then also state that the model never writes it);
- who undresses whom;
- POV and tense;
- act menu and rotation, executable form: one act fills at most one slot per scene; the same combination does not repeat next scene unless the player asks;
- intensity bands: valid inside one scene, reset after it, no jump to the top band;
- who leads: fixed by the character, or chosen by the player (player picks one side, the character takes the other);
- narration vocabulary: the direct words that fit the character and the scene; no detached euphemism, no clinical wording; never force a word they would not use;
- onomatopoeia module: optional, default OFF; when ON, a closed lexicon supplied by the author, active only during intimate scenes, frequency set only by the author.

### 10. Climax and continuation
Climax is a state change, not an automatic END SCENE. Next legal directions: continued interaction; lower-intensity contact; change of focus or role; recovery inside the scene; renewed escalation after recovery; character-specific aftermath; scene end. Choose from the character's personality, current body state, preferences, player action, relationship context, and scene conditions. Never impose "every scene needs aftercare", "one climax ends the scene", "everyone must climax", or "climax causes confession, trust, or tenderness".

### 11. Scenario-Based NSFW Differentiation
When the same character's adult behavior should differ meaningfully across relationship states, scenes, routes, public/private conditions, or author-defined personas (alters, masks, transformed forms, a split personality), compile those differences instead of averaging them into one generic mode. Each branch defines its trigger, initiative/control, body/action priorities, voice fragmentation, pacing, and own-pleasure logic when relevant. Whatever the canon keeps stable stays shared; an author-defined persona keeps its own canon voice.
A persona the model invents for adult scenes is not a branch; it is the personality-swap failure (§16.2).
One context: APD plus relevant ED is enough source. Route-dependent differentiation needs the full card. A card meant for other group chats gets an observable condition in ED that selects the correct branch.
Multi-partner scenes are outside this module's scope, not outside the builder's ability; they use the ordinary compilation rules.

### 12. Bounded novelty
Fresh behavior extends or recombines the established grammar instead of switching to a generic repertoire. A few strong anchors plus wide recombination space. Omitted behaviors are available by causality, not forbidden; only explicit exclusions are closed. Variety lives across scenes, never packed into one. When playtest shows repetition, adjust one dimension (initiative, geometry, focus, pacing, sensory emphasis, voice, prop use, aftermath) instead of adding "be more varied".

### 13. Pattern grammar (author-side source; never pasted into a card)
Use this as a source library, not a checklist. For each candidate: install, strip for parts, recombine, or reject. Choose by character logic, never trope/gender/visual design. A useful anchor usually combines `trigger -> interaction style -> geometry/focus -> response -> meaning -> variation`.
- **Initiation**: direct, invited, teasing, reactive, waiting, redirecting, challenge-based, alternating, ritualized, opportunistic.
- **Control / reciprocity**: directive, playful, competitive, negotiated, soft-guided, improvisational, surrendered, alternating; control may live in pace, placement, gaze, clothing, or timing.
- **Geometry**: facing/avoiding eye contact, side-by-side, turned away, standing, seated, braced, low-mobility, repositioning-heavy, clothing-mediated; choose for leverage, visibility, closeness, mobility, comfort.
- **Body / material focus**: body areas relevant to the character; whole-body pressure; fabric-covered vs exposed; garments kept on, displaced, removed, damaged, or restored when in character.
- **Rhythm / senses**: slow build, fast start, teasing delay, pulsed, stop-start, plateau, abrupt redirection; visual, tactile, temperature, texture, sound/breath, balance/fatigue, micro-reactions.
- **Verbal modes**: blunt, precise, teasing, competitive, affectionate, possessive, technical, understated, vulgar if in character, near-silent, self-conscious, joking, formal, nicknames, broken syntax with clear intent.
- **Transitions / aftermath**: intensify, plateau, slow, redirect, change geometry, pause, shift initiative, lower effort, resume, end; aftermath may be routine, teasing, pride, awkwardness, affection, practical care, distance, non-discussion, renewed activity, sleep. No automatic therapy or meaning.

### 14. Control rules (source-driven or user-requested only)
Controls already in the original card (consent method, delegated control, safeword or stop, aftercare, or others) are inherited as written. Do not propose a new control merely because the card is explicit. The builder may propose compiling one only when an existing premise, source rule, or requested behavior clearly requires an operational control layer; preserve the source intent and do not invent the control itself. Otherwise new controls are added only when the user asks.
- Consent method: one ask, negotiated beforehand, a standing arrangement, or the card's own rule; never more than the user requested.
- Delegated control (CNC, hypnosis, contract, transformation and similar premises): separate what authority is fictionally delegated; what stays under player authorship; bodily or mechanical effects; emotional meaning; any requested stop; and the out-of-character agreement itself. Never infer the player's thoughts, desire, consent state, or relationship meaning from a bodily effect.
- Safeword or stop: exactly the word and scope the user gives.
- Aftercare: only the order and content the user gives.

## 15. Example-writing craft (samples, Greeting patterns)
- Density order: voice -> body sensation or state -> what it changes in them -> next action.
- Density comes from the character's body, contact, attention, and voice, never from writing the player's actions.
- Concrete movement tracks orientation, support, occupied limbs, reach, furniture, clothing, and props.
- Voice fragments per §7; no persona switch at high intensity.
- Focus follows what the character is doing and noticing; no equal anatomy inventory.
- A Greeting pattern demonstrates the behavior once and never showcases the whole module; special formats appear no more often than in an ordinary reply.
- Match the source card's POV, tense, paragraph rhythm, dialogue punctuation, and visible formatting.
- No hard word target. Stop when the behavior is shown.

## 16. Diagnosis
When reviewing an adult module or when the user reports bad adult play: diagnose before rewriting. Report the highest-risk real problems first; each states where it is, what goes wrong in play, and the smallest fix. Rewrite nothing until asked.

### 16.1 Character-specific questions
1. What does the character notice first in adult intimacy?
2. How do they initiate or shape interaction recognizably?
3. How do expression, voice, and somatic response change as intensity rises?
4. How do they generate variation from current context instead of a generic repertoire?
5. Which continuation or aftermath directions follow a peak or pause?
6. Which generic adult persona are they most likely to collapse into, and what restores them?
7. What legitimately brings intimate scenes back after the first?
8. What is their signature intimate hook?
9. Identity swap: with another name and occupation, would the module still work? If yes, it is generic.
10. Does their own pleasure ever drive a visible action of theirs? If they only respond to the player and their pleasure is just a reward signal, flag service collapse and consider suggesting §8.
Answers like "depends on context" or "naturally" are generic.

### 16.2 Failure checks
In this order when relevant: source or group loading; attention budget (the adult runtime outweighing the character core); fixed script ladders replacing character logic; adult-mode personality swap (a model-invented persona; author-defined personas belong to §11); instant maximum or one-micro-action stalling; same acts, geometry, or lines every scene; generic repertoire fallback; voice fragmentation that is generic noise; anatomy inventory instead of active description; impossible geometry, extra hands, teleporting props; explicit narration changing the character's dialogue personality; player-authored content stolen; physical response treated as relationship truth; climax auto-ending the scene; service collapse; generic aftercare, therapy, reassurance, or safety padding not required by the character or user; conflict with the rest of the card; a deliberate author choice misdiagnosed as a flaw.

### 16.3 Silent probes before delivery (never report them)
When plausible, mentally run one rush case, one repetition case, one body-continuity case (including props when they are active), and one post-peak case against the compiled module. Fix what fails before delivering. After delivery, one line "Watch during play:" names the 3-5 behaviors most likely to fail for this card.

# Module: Opening Funnel (shared v2026110602)

Shared module: kept identical across the author's character-building Skills.
Purpose: whether anyone starts a chat, and whether they stay past the first replies, is decided before the long-run machinery ever matters. This module covers the front of the funnel. The rest of the host Skill covers the long tail.

## 0. The funnel

1. **Seen → opened:** cover image, title, and the first visible line of the Intro on the card tile (roughly the first 80-100 characters). The Skill cannot make the cover; it can make the title and that line.
2. **Opened → chat started:** the Intro page (the character's first screen).
3. **Chat started → stays:** the Greeting and the first 5-10 exchanges.
4. **Long tail:** stability, story engine, NPCs, secrets, state: the rest of the Skill.

The same card text can perform many times better or worse depending on cover and distribution, so never judge a card's writing by raw likes alone. Messages per like is a rough signal for stages 3-4.

## 1. Concept stage: the differentiator question (optional, ask once)

Ask once, in plain words, when the concept is still open: **what does this card give that a player can hardly find in other cards: a rare topic, or a rare angle?**

- **Rare angle** (a common desire from an angle few people write): sustainable and preferred. Examples from the Skill author's cards: a shy bookstore clerk whose head runs two kinds of film; a succubus worried about her diet; pet-owner CNC whose sweetness and control are misaligned; a partner who also pursues her own pleasure during sex (common in life, rare in RP).
- **Rare topic** (content many like to read and few write, e.g. tentacles): can break out fast, but tends to escalate toward shock. Fine when chosen; then check that the long tail has an engine beyond the topic.
- Never push a concept toward the bizarre just to be different. If the user has no answer, drop the question.

## 2. Title

- Names the situation or the hook, not only the character: `Name: <hook>` or `Name | <hook>`.
- Short enough to survive truncation on the tile.

## 3. The tile line (the Intro's first sentence)

The Intro's first sentence is what the tile shows under the title. It must work alone as the ad.

- Second person, one charged fact or a situation inversion that flips what the player assumes. Lines from the Skill author's cards: "It's not you controlling the tentacles — it's her." / "Your gentle wife, your yandere rival." / "You were sent to fight your wife."
- Do not spend it on a tag list or on repeating the title. If the card uses genre tags, keep them short and after the hook, or leave them to the platform's tag fields.
- Check: read only the first ~90 characters. Would a stranger know what is unusual here, and want to know more?

## 4. Intro (the first screen)

- Sells what the player will actually do and feel in this chat, not a biography.
- Second person. Short sentences and fragments are welcome. Concrete, memorable specifics beat adjectives ("keeps a second toothbrush and swears it belongs to nobody" beats "beautiful, intelligent, fashionable").
- Show the character through one or two behaviors, not a trait list.
- Optional roll-call for an ensemble: bold `Name, age, role.` plus one telling line each.
- Stakes in a line or two; end on a line that sets the tone, or one easy question.
- When explicit adult play is installed, one plain line makes the adult premise clear; the card's kind should never be a surprise.
- Secrets follow `secrets-and-reveals.md` (hidden by default; revealed only by the user's choice).
- An enabled interaction control (safeword) may get a concise player-facing notice; the operative rule stays in ED.
- Target about 1,600 characters (cap 2,000). The Intro is never the only home of a runtime rule.
- The Intro is not loaded into group chats and is not read by the runtime; it carries nothing load-bearing.

## 5. Greeting

- Performs the exact opening once: the character's judgment, action or speech, and real room for the player to answer. Never authors non-inferable player content.
- **Charged hook:** beyond setting up the plot, at least one element the player wants to answer: tension, want, stakes, a choice only they can settle. When it stops at a gate (a consent question, a decision), the heat is built before the gate (sensory detail, effects the premise imposes, the character's visible want and restraint), never by starting what the gate asks about.
- **Playable ending:** end on something concrete and easy to answer: a direct question, a request, an interruption, a choice.
- **Style anchor:** the Greeting is the strongest style example in the card; the runtime imitates it every turn, more than any rule. Write it as one strong ordinary turn, not a showcase. Replies drift toward its length, its balance of narration, speech, and inner voice, and how often each special format appears. No author voice in the character's mouth, no runtime explanation, no habit you would not want every turn.
- Follows every output format ED defines exactly; ends with the initial state record when the card has an internal tracker (`state-tracking.md`).
- After delivering a Greeting, ask the user to check that its style and point of view are what they want.

## 6. The first 5-10 exchanges

- **Opening beats:** Scenario carries two or three beat candidates reachable within the first few exchanges, grown from the opening situation. Candidates, not a railway.
- **Own agenda early:** by roughly the second to fifth reply the character does something of their own (pursues a want, reacts to the world, starts something) without waiting for the player to push.
- **Early pull:** within the first few exchanges, something the player came for (intimacy, conflict, closeness, a reveal path) becomes reachable, not granted.
- **Hook object:** when the card has a secret or a shared past, one visible object, habit, or unchanged detail can carry it (see `secrets-and-reveals.md`).
- No long setup monologue in the first replies; information arrives through action.

## 7. Pull check

Every rule that holds back something the player came for must have a matching engine that legitimately produces it. A card made only of brakes plays safe and flat. The Intro states the real appeal; the Greeting holds a charged hook; with explicit adult play installed, a recurring intimacy driver exists (see the adult module).

## 8. Checks before delivery

- Title names the hook; the tile line stands alone.
- Intro: second person, concrete, within ~1,600 characters, carries nothing load-bearing.
- Greeting: charged hook, playable ending, demonstrates no defect, formats at ordinary frequency.
- Scenario has opening beat candidates; the character's own agenda can surface early.
- Pull check passes.
